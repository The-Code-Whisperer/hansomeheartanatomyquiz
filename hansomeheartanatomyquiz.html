🎯 Quick Test

Q: What valve separates RA from RV?
Tricuspid valve (right AV valve) — has 3 cusps: anterior, posterior, septal
Q: What's the function of the moderator band?
Contains right bundle branch of AV node → conducts impulse to anterior papillary muscle (prevents valve prolapse)
Q: Where is the fossa ovalis located?
Interatrial septum, right atrium — remnant of fetal foramen ovale (shunt between atria)
  • Can draw heart external features from memory
  • Know all structures entering/leaving right atrium
  • Can trace blood flow through right side
  • Know all valves and their cusp numbers

🎯 Quick Test

Q: Which aortic arch branch gives rise to the right subclavian?
Brachiocephalic trunk (1st branch) — it bifurcates into right subclavian and right common carotid
Q: What artery supplies the thyroid gland?
Superior thyroid (external carotid) and Inferior thyroid (thyrocervical trunk from subclavian)
Q: Which subclavian branch goes to the brain?
Vertebral artery — ascends through transverse foramina of C6-C1, enters foramen magnum

  • Can list 4 subclavian branches
  • Know carotid bifurcation level and branches

  • 🩸 Internal carotid arteries (proximal part)

4️⃣

Arch 4 — ASYMMETRIC!

⚠️ Click for R vs L

Arch 4: Right vs Left

  • 👉 RIGHT:
       • Proximal right subclavian
  • 👈 LEFT:
       • Aortic arch (between left common carotid and left subclavian)
  • 🔤 Nerve: Vagus (CN X) + Superior laryngeal
  • 💪 Muscles: Cricothyroid, pharyngeal constrictors
6️⃣

Arch 6

Click for derivatives

Arch 6 Derivatives

  • 🩸 Pulmonary arteries (right and left)
  • 🩸 Ductus arteriosus (ligamentum arteriosum)
  • 🔤 Nerve: Recurrent laryngeal (branch of vagus)
  • ⚠️ R vs L difference:
       • Right: loops under right subclavian
       • Left: loops under aortic arch

🚨 EXAM TRAP: R vs L Differences

Q: What does the 4th arch form on the RIGHT?
Proximal part of the RIGHT SUBCLAVIAN ARTERY
Q: What does the 4th arch form on the LEFT?
The AORTIC ARCH (between left common carotid and left subclavian)
Q: Why does left recurrent laryngeal nerve loop lower?
It hooks under the 6th arch (ductus arteriosus) which becomes ligamentum arteriosum — on the left this is lower because the 4th arch becomes the aortic arch
  • Know Arch 1 = Maxillary artery
  • Know Arch 3 = Carotids
  • Know Arch 4 R vs L difference cold
  • Know Arch 6 = Pulmonary + ductus arteriosus
  • Understand why recurrent laryngeal nerves differ

Superior Vena Cava

Click for tributaries

SVC Formation & Tributaries

  • 📍 Formed by union of:
       • Right brachiocephalic vein
       • Left brachiocephalic vein
  • 📍 Enters right atrium (superior aspect)
  • 📍 Azygos vein drains into SVC (posterior)
  • 📍 No valves
⬆️

Inferior Vena Cava

Click for key facts

IVC Key Facts

  • 📍 Formed at L5 (union of common iliacs)
  • 📍 Enters right atrium (inferior aspect)
  • 📍 Passes through diaphragm (T8)
  • 📍 Hepatic veins drain directly into IVC
  • 📍 Renal veins (L longer than R)
🫁

Pulmonary Trunk

Click for bifurcation

Pulmonary Circulation

  • 📍 Arises from right ventricle
  • 📍 Bifurcates at T5-T7 level
  • 📍 Right pulmonary artery (longer, crosses aorta)
  • 📍 Left pulmonary artery (shorter, anterior to descending aorta)
  • 📍 Ligamentum arteriosum connects left PA to aortic arch

🎯 Relations to Heart

Q: Where does SVC enter the heart?
Superior aspect of the right atrium — brings deoxygenated blood from upper body
Q: What structure connects left pulmonary artery to aorta?
Ligamentum arteriosum — remnant of fetal ductus arteriosus (shunt from pulmonary trunk to aorta)
Q: Which pulmonary artery crosses the aorta?
Right pulmonary artery — it passes anterior to the ascending aorta and posterior to the SVC
  • Know SVC tributaries and entry point
  • Know IVC formation level and entry
  • Can trace pulmonary trunk bifurcation
  • Know ligamentum arteriosum significance

🔬 Histology (10% of exam)

Exam Priority: LOWER but easy points | Focus on vessel walls

Vessel Wall Layers "Tunica Intima, Media, Adventitia" IMA — Intima (inner), Media (middle), Adventitia (outer)
🧅

Tunica Intima

Click for layers

Innermost Layer

  • 🧱 Endothelium (simple squamous)
  • 🧱 Subendothelial layer (connective tissue)
  • 🧱 Internal elastic lamina (arteries)
  • 💡 Thinnest layer
  • 💡 Smooth surface reduces friction
💪

Tunica Media

Click for composition

Middle Layer (Thickest in Arteries)

  • 🧱 Smooth muscle cells (circular)
  • 🧱 Elastic fibers (especially in large arteries)
  • 🧱 Collagen fibers
  • 🧱 External elastic lamina
  • 💡 Regulates vessel diameter
🕸️

Tunica Adventitia

Click for features

Outermost Layer

  • 🧱 Connective tissue (collagen)
  • 🧱 Elastic fibers
  • 🧱 Vasa vasorum (vessels to vessel wall)
  • 🧱 Nervi vascularis (nerves)
  • 💡 Anchors vessel to surrounding tissue
  • 💡 Thickest layer in veins

🆚 Artery vs Vein Histology

Q: Which layer is thickest in arteries?
Tunica media — contains smooth muscle for maintaining blood pressure
Q: Which layer is thickest in veins?
Tunica adventitia — veins have thin media, need adventitia for structural support
Q: What is the vasa vasorum?
"Vessels of vessels" — small blood vessels in tunica adventitia that supply the outer half of large vessel walls (aorta, vena cava)
  • Can name 3 layers in order
  • Know intima = endothelium + internal elastic lamina
  • Know media = smooth muscle (thickest in arteries)
  • Know adventitia = connective tissue + vasa vasorum
  • Know differences between artery and vein walls
